## 1. Trains, railways, stations and rail travel: do PZ mods add them, how do they work, how popular are they?

### Takeaway
Until July 2026 Project Zomboid had **no working train mod**. Community threads from 2023 to January 2025 asked for one and got nothing. Rail content was limited to static map mods such as "Train Base near MD" and a roadside railroad house. I read only their titles and tags. The one real train mod is **Railroader** by SlavaPo. It is B42-only and was published 2026-07-30, one day after B42 went stable. On 2026-10-05 it had **41,326 current subscribers** (59,383 lifetime). It already has a small add-on ecosystem. One add-on, **Doomsday Railroader**, turns the locomotive into a road-capable moving base with 8×16 "living cars", which is the closest existing analog to the steam-train-as-moving-base concept, but it is single-player only and has under 1,000 subscribers. I found no handcar, rail-cart, draisine or station-to-station travel mod.

### Inferences
- Railroader confirms the **"authored rail graph + switch nodes" model** instead of free vehicle physics. It runs a non-vehicle entity along authored routes laid over existing track geometry, with explicit switches. That maps directly onto an OpenRailwayMap pipeline: OSM rail ways become authored route polylines, junctions become switch nodes, and stations and depots become stop nodes.
- Persistent scarcity makes the train feel like a character. There is one locomotive per world, its state is randomized on discovery, and diesel comes only from finite tanks at named depots. For a steam train, the equivalents would be water towers and coal or wood stocks tied to specific stations, which gives each stop a logistic reason to exist beyond loot.
- **Failure states create expeditions.** Derailment is a four-hour, failable job, and the tools for it are stocked in railroad buildings. That turns trackside buildings into objectives, which is a template for "station stops have a purpose beyond scavenging" and for short detachments.
- "Parked, she is a wall" means a stopped train becomes defensive terrain at a station. That suggests stop-layout design where the train shields one side of the platform.
- Moving bases and multiplayer anti-cheat collide. Railroader needs `AntiCheatSpeed=4`, RV Interior asks admins to disable anti-cheat types 2 and 12 (Section 2), and Aquatsar's title tells MP users to disable anti-cheat type 12. Any online feature that puts players on a moving platform should design server-side movement validation in from the start.
- Interiors are implemented as **separate instanced map areas** (Doomsday cars, RV Interior), not as walkable moving geometry. REF's walkway is the single-player exception. This is the standard engineering compromise. On mobile, a scene switch into the "train interior" is the natural equivalent, and "stop before entering" is an acceptable rule.
- Engagement looks niche but intense. Favorites run about 9.9% of current subscribers (4,078 / 41,326), against roughly 3.5% for FR Used Cars (65,026 / 1,873,871) and 2.7% for KI5's M911 (54,735 / 2,039,286). This is my own ratio calculation, and I treat favorite ratio only as a rough enthusiasm proxy.
- A solo developer using AI-assisted code reached a multiplayer-capable 2.x in about six weeks. But Railroader leans entirely on vanilla's existing track and world. A standalone game also has to build the world, so the feasibility signal applies to the rail system, not to the whole product.

- I couldn't get Railroader's exact version history (1.0 → 2.0 → 2.1) or the contents of its change notes, because the changelog page returned HTTP 429.
- As of the 2026-09-13 description, rolling stock (freight cars, caboose, live-in business car) was still listed as "road ahead". I don't know whether wagons shipped after that.

## 2. Vehicles, trailers and vehicle interiors; snow vehicles, sleds, handcars and rail carts

### Takeaway
Vehicle mods and their frameworks are among the most-subscribed items on the PZ Workshop. **Tsar's Common Library 2.0** had 4.26M current subscribers, the largest number I saw. KI5's M998 had 3.22M and KI5's framework "that DAMN Library" 3.10M. **Autotsar Trailers** had 2.46M, KI5's **M911 + semi-trailers** 2.04M, **Filibuster Rhymes' Used Cars** 1.87M, **RV Interior** 1.72M and KI5's **Trailers!** 1.61M. KI5 kept its packs on B41+B42 itself. Autotsar, FR Used Cars and RV Interior reached B42 through third-party ports. Mobile-base content is a big draw: semi-trailers sold as "move your whole base", camper trailers, and enterable vehicle interiors. **Sleds, snowmobiles and handcars are essentially absent from PZ.** The only "sled" I found is an old Hydrocraft item. The closest modern analog is pushable carts: ZuperCart (B41, 1.05M) and SaucedCarts (B42, 93k).

### Inferences

- RV Interior **ties interior utilities to the vehicle**. A train design could carry this over: carriage heat, light and water depend on the locomotive and boiler, and a detached car or sled loses them. That is a built-in source of tension for detachments.
- The cart rules make a good template for **sleds and handcar detachments**: one per person, two-handed, roughly 49–50 capacity, no fence climbing or driving, and dropped the moment you aim. Hauling capacity is traded directly against combat and mobility.
- Vanilla 42.20's "trailers can now detach" at sharp turns or high speed shows the base game modelling coupling risk. A train game could do the same with decoupling on hard braking or derailment.
- KI5's model of modular, physical parts ("All parts have 3d objects you can remove and place", armor plates, run-flat tires, findable extras) suggests treating locomotive and car upgrades (plow, armor, stove) as looted parts.
- Third-party ports carried the big B41 vehicle content (Autotsar, FR, RV Interior) into B42. That shows how much these systems depend on frameworks such as Tsar's library and that DAMN Library. This doesn't apply directly to a standalone mobile game, but it explains why the B41 originals still show larger subscriber numbers.

## 3. Real-place map mods: which are famous, how mappers trade real geography for playability, interiors and loot, and whether procedural maps exist

### Takeaway
The most-subscribed map mods are mostly **fictional towns**: Raven Creek 1.71M, Bedford Falls 1.38M, Grapeseed 1.28M, Fort Redstone 1.22M, Blackwood 1.18M. Real-place maps are fewer but present: West Point Expansion 758k, Ekron 729k, Fort Benning 380k, Rabbit Hash 303k, and Fort Knox 159k plus 293k for its Eerie-linked variant. Real-place mappers start from Google Maps, then **displace, compress and invent** to fit the cell grid and the road network. The vanilla map does the same: Knox Country is a loose rendering of the Fort Knox–Louisville area. **B42 doubled the map's surface area** westward with towns named after real places (Brandenburg, Ekron, Irvington). That broke older map mods' coordinates and caused at least one name collision. There is **no runtime procedural map generation** in PZ, only offline generators that produce map projects. The only procedural element in vanilla is **400 procedural basements alongside 75 unique ones**.

### Inferences
- PZ practice supports building **station surroundings as "anchored but compressed"** layouts. Keep the real anchors: station name, river, highway and track topology from OpenRailwayMap. Then fill with authored prefab blocks, and allow displacement where it suits the grid. Rabbit Hash was moved "a fair ways down the river" because it fit the cells and roads. Fort Knox needed invented filler, including a piece of a Georgia town. Even vanilla is "loosely based".
- **Prefab library plus unique landmarks** has official precedent: B42 ships 400 procedural basements next to 75 unique ones. The 42.20 revamp also pushes "All buildings and houses are now unique", which tells you players notice repetition. A prefab-block station system should give every station a signature unique piece, such as the station building, a landmark or a local business.
- **Raid zones versus base zones.** Raven Creek was "planned for fast raids … not to establish a base", with sub-zones of different difficulty and heavy loot. That matches a train game directly: stations are raid zones and the train is the base. Per-zone difficulty (Raven Creek, Eerie Country's A–D zones) maps to per-station danger ratings.
- **Information as loot.** Bedford Falls' lootable map updates the world map, and West Point's Salt Ridge map spawns in gas stations. A station-area map found on a previous visit or in a neighboring town could reveal the prefab layout and the danger before arrival.
- **Absolute coordinates are fragile.** The B42 map change made Autotsar's trailer spawn points "obsolete", forced Raven Creek's relocation, gave Grapeseed a new cell system, and pushed the Ekron mod toward a rename right as vanilla added its own Ekron. That last link is my inference; the mod doesn't state the reason. A world generated from OpenRailwayMap should give stations and blocks stable IDs that are separate from coordinates, so re-imports and map updates don't break saves or content.
- The fact that most of the top-subscribed maps are fictional suggests that **density of interesting content beats geographic fidelity** for players. Real geography is a flavor layer, not the selling point.

## 4. Winter and cold: vanilla temperature system, challenges and mods

### Takeaway
Vanilla PZ simulates **core body temperature** from clothing insulation, wind resistance, wetness, windchill, activity and nearby heat. The hypothermia moodle has four tiers that trigger below 36.5, 35, 30 and 25 °C **[snippet-only]**. But vanilla Kentucky winters are **mild**: about −5 °C for a few weeks in January even on the "Cold" preset, according to a mod author. Powered buildings also stay warm. Winter mods therefore pull a handful of levers. They push outdoor temperatures far lower (Cryogenic Winter: −60/−80/−155 °C winter lows depending on preset; Cold Winters: down to −30 °C). They force power off so buildings lose their "magic heating". They simulate indoor heat flow with leaks and heaters (Realistic Temperature). They freeze water and food (Below Freezing). And they add readable snow traces (Immersive Snow). Winter overhauls are a **niche** at roughly 60k–145k current subscribers. The winter utility "Snow is water" reached **1.02M**, and its feature is now vanilla in B42.

### Cited Findings

#### Vanilla temperature system (high level)
- Hypothermia moodle tiers **[snippet-only]**: Chilly below 36.5 °C, no effect. Cold below 35 °C, "34% decreased attack speed", moderately slower movement. Freezing below 30 °C, 67% decreased attack speed. Hypothermic below 25 °C, "90% decreased attack speed. Severely decreased walking/running speed. Slowly loses health until at 1% Health." Causes include "lack of warm clothes, staying wet, cold weather/wind chill or a vehicle's A/C". Remedies are warmer clothes, staying indoors, a campfire or the vehicle heater. — [PZwiki Hypothermia](https://pzwiki.net/wiki/Hypothermia). Another snippet of the same page gives 33% instead of 34% for the Cold tier.
- "Factors such as insulation, wind resistance and wetness (body and clothing) determine heat retention and heat loss", and "Wind resistance reduces the impact of windchill" **[snippet-only]**. — [PZwiki Clothing](https://pzwiki.net/wiki/Armor). Windchill: "the apparent temperature may be colder than the actual air temperature. - **Below Freezing 零度以下** (B41): 188 current, so tiny. It is still a full design spec. Power and water are cut immediately. Water barrels and fountains freeze "unless a campfire is lit next to the barrels". Food and water are frozen and need heating. "rivers and lakes are frozen over and you can't fish, but you can walk on the ice". You can take snow from the ground or ice from lakes and melt it in a pot over a campfire. Foraging and trapping are reduced, crops struggle, and food spoils more slowly. It forces a December start. — [Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3200490725)
- Smaller B41 mods. **Permafrost** (8,107) lets you set min/max temperatures and "ALMOST permanent" snowfall — [Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2712620992). **Eternal Snow** (10,281) runs −10 °C by day and −20 °C at night — [Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2686791433). **Infinite Weather** (16,871) has a "Ceaseless Snow" mode where "The winter lasts forever … Occasionally there are severe, intense blizzards" — [Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2086715842).
- **The Eternaut - DEADLY SNOW** (3,956): "If you take off even a single piece of clothing while it's snowing, you will die immediately — worn-out clothing counts too". You're safe inside buildings, or "in vehicles, as long as all windows and doors are closed". Its title says B41/B42+ but its only tag is B41. — [Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3475135570)

#### Snow utility and snow atmosphere
- **Snow is water [B41.74 Legacy]**: **1,024,860 current / 2,029,723 lifetime**. Rain barrels and metal barrels "are filled up when it snows at a slower rate (50%)". "This mod won't be updated for b42 since this feature is already a part of that version of the game." — [Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2704811006)
- **Immersive Snow** (B42, by elu): **59,643 current after 6 days** (created 2026-09-29). It has footprints for players, zombies and animals (including "Horse Mod horses"), tire ruts with tread patterns, "trampled paths where a horde keeps walking", snow piling on vehicles and clothes, "Falling and melting snow soaks your clothes", breath vapour, frost on windows that you scrape, snow that "thaws around fires", and "frost on the screen in hypothermia". — [Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3810071828)

### Inferences
- For a "deadly winter" premise, PZ shows that **vanilla-style mild winters are too forgiving** and what to tune: outdoor temperature range presets, a permanent-snow toggle, removing free building heat, indoor heat leakage, and water freezing. A steam train fits naturally as the heat economy. The boiler is the heat source, carriages leak heat through open doors, and stations have no free warmth unless you fuel a stove there. Cryogenic Winter's "magic heated house" complaint is the thing to avoid.
- **Gradual, tunable lethality beats instant death.** Cryogenic (126k), with its Easy/Normal/Hard presets, far outperforms The Eternaut's instant death on exposure (4k). For a mobile market, difficulty presets for temperature are probably essential. That's my inference from subscriber counts.
- **Uncertain weather information** is a proven layer: Unseasonal Weather's radio forecasts that can be false or interrupted (82k). That fits expedition and overnight-camp decisions such as "is a blizzard coming before the detachment returns?".
- **Snow as readable information**, from Immersive Snow: footprints, trampled horde paths, blood trails. In a top-down game this can telegraph threats (a horde's path across the snow) and show where detachments have gone. Immersive Snow's fast adoption in late September 2026 (59.6k in six days) shows how much players want winter readability.
- Winter maintenance of vehicles (Dynamic Snow's staged buildup, engine heat only partly melting it, shelter mattering) could become train and sled upkeep at stops: clearing snow, choosing covered sheds. That's my inference.
- Hard numbers to borrow: PZ's four-tier hypothermia ladder at roughly 36.5/35/30/25 °C, with attack and movement penalties scaling up and health drain only at the last tier **[snippet-only]**, is a clear, readable status model for mobile UI.

### Gaps
- I didn't get **numeric insulation or wind-resistance values** for vanilla clothing, or the vanilla indoor-temperature formula, because PZwiki was unreachable.
- The hypothermia percentages are snippet-only, and the two snippets disagree (33% vs 34% for the Cold tier).
- I don't know whether vanilla B42 freezes water sources or food. Below Freezing's feature list implies it doesn't, but I couldn't confirm that.
- I don't know the specifics of "Winter is Coming" in B42.20+; the guide data is B41-era and snippet-only. I also don't know what the new "Top of the World" challenge contains.
- I couldn't get the full Cold Winters description (truncated by the API excerpt), including any non-temperature features.

## 5. Camps and bases: camping, campfires, tents, base defense and barricades

### Takeaway
According to modders, **vanilla tents are mostly storage or decoration** in B41/B42. B42 adds lean-to, tarp and hide shelters to the build menu and puts a **fuel limit on campfires**. Mods that make camping functional are small: Camp in the Rain has 46.5k current and 124k lifetime subscribers, the backpack shelter 6.3k, and the campfire fuel unlocker 1.2k. Base-defense mods that make defenses durable and maintainable are much bigger: Repair Wall 360k, Barricade Hurt Zombies 205k. Vehicle-based mobile bases dwarf both (Section 2: RV Interior 1.72M plus 741k for B42, Campers! 796k).

#### Mobile bases (see Section 2)
- RV Interior (1.72M B41 / 741k B42), KI5 Campers! (796k), Autotsar's camper trailer, KI5's M911 "move your whole base", and Doomsday Railroader's living cars (930, Section 1). — [RV Interior](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/?id=2822286426), [Project RV Interior](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3543229299), [Campers!](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3670064951)

### Inferences
- **Camps protect against weather, not zombies.** Camp in the Rain explicitly doesn't protect from zombies, and heavy storms still soak you. That's a good default for overnight expedition camps: a camp cuts cold and wetness but creates a watch and defense problem. Camping then becomes a risk decision, not a free rest.
- The one-item backpack shelter shows players want **camp kits as single inventory items**, which suits detachment loadouts on sleds or handcars.
- The campfire fuel limit in vanilla B42, and a mod to remove it, show that fuel management is contentious. A camp fuel cap should be readable and explained in the UI. That's an inference from the existence of the unlock mod.
- **Maintainable defenses** draw several times more subscribers than tent mods (Repair Wall at 360k, Barricade Hurt Zombies at 205k, against roughly 46k for Camp in the Rain). For a train, armor plating and car walls that wear down and get cheap repairs at stops fits what players have shown they want.
- **The moving base is the main fantasy and the camp is the supporting one.** Subscriber counts for vehicle bases and interiors are 15 to 40 times those of camping mods. This supports making optional camps lightweight rather than a second base-building system.

### Gaps
- I didn't verify vanilla B42 tent or shelter stats (rain, cold and rest effects) from a primary source; the claims come from the Camp in the Rain author.
- I didn't research big general base-building mods (the "More Builds" kind) or vanilla B42 changes to barricade and wall durability. The 2026-05-29 post titled "REINFORCING THE BARRICADES" turned out to be about localization and controllers, not defenses.
- I found no data on vanilla safehouse rules or on how zombies are drawn to campfire light and noise.
